# Pit-stop count is the race minimum plus linked extra stops

The original count model drew each car's stops as an independent Poisson count. That let it predict outcomes the sporting rules and the data rule out. The COTA preset gave a zero-stop team total in 7% of draws, but 0 of 475 fit rows had 0 stops. It also drew a team's two cars independently, yet in 2026 rounds 1–17 both cars made the same number of stops in 79 of 118 team-races (67%).

The model now treats a running car's stops as the race minimum plus a Poisson count of extra stops, and the GLM is fitted on `stops − minimum`. The minimum comes from the `stop_rules` dbt seed (Monaco from 2025 = 2; every other dry race 1). Mixed and wet races also use 1, which the user set on 2026-10-21 after 0 of 35 mixed-race finishers made zero stops. Circuit names stay out of analysis code. A finisher below its minimum fails the fit instead of being silently clipped. The two cars share one uniform draw with probability ρ and take the Poisson inverse CDF of it. ρ is set in closed form so the in-sample same-count share matches the observed one (0.547 for COTA 2026). This keeps each car's own Poisson marginal, and so its start-tyre effect, exactly. Copying one car's count to the other would erase that effect.

Why, as recorded: the user's call during story 7 planning ("we have to change model if it gives an impossible answer like 0"), in `change-race-minimum-and-team-plans` (2026-10-21) and the story 8 plan. The trade-off: the 2026 backtest's overall MAE stayed at 1.70, but incident-race MAE rose to 1.93, which failed the earlier incident keep rule. That rule was waived for this change on bias grounds (the old model under-predicted incident races by 0.74; this one by 0.31). Known limits: two finishers in the committed data made 0 stops (Ocon, Istanbul 2021; Gasly, Abu Dhabi 2019), so summarising those circuits needs a seed row or a rule change first. The forecast's same-count share has not been checked against 67%.
