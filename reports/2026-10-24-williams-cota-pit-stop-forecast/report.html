Williams pit stops: 2026 United States Grand Prix

How many pit stops will Williams make in total at the Circuit of the Americas? Round 18, Sunday 2026-10-25

2026-10-24

The answer

Williams are expected to make 3.2 [2–6] pit stops in total at the 2026 United States Grand Prix (Circuit of the Americas, round 18, 2026-10-25), counting both cars and every stop.

The first number is the average over 10,000 simulated races; the two in brackets are the range: in at least 8 of 10 simulated races the team made between those two numbers of stops, inclusive. This is the most likely scenario: dry weather, with the cars starting on SOFT (C4) + MEDIUM (C3).

That total already allows for a 50% chance of a safety car or red flag, from how often one came out in earlier races at this circuit and earlier races this season.

  • With no safety car or red flag: 3.0 [2–5] stops.
  • With a safety car or red flag: 3.5 [2–6] stops.

For comparison, two simple rules of thumb for this race: the circuit’s history (two cars × its stops per car since 2011) gives 3.1 stops, and the team’s season so far (two cars × its stops per car this season) gives 4.5.

How well the model did this season

Each earlier round of 2026 was forecast for every team from what was known before it, and each forecast was compared with the team’s actual total. Circuit history had the lowest mean absolute error, 1.56 stops, 0.14 stops below the model (1.70). The model’s p10–p90 range held the actual total in 81% of the 187 team-races, against a target of 80%; the range was 4.2 stops wide on average.

Model notes

At this race a running car makes at least 1 stop, in any weather. In 55% of the draws the team’s two cars share one draw for their extra stops, so they tend to make the same number; in the rest each car draws its own.   • Penalty stops with no tyre change cannot be told apart from other stops in the data, so they are counted as normal stops.
  • The wet and mixed ranges rest on few races, so they are wider: every draw samples the model’s coefficients from their estimated uncertainty, and a level seen in few races is uncertain.
  • The start-tyre effect is a pattern in past races, not cause and effect: teams choose their start tyres for reasons that also change how often they stop.
  • A circuit or team with no stop history before the race uses the field mean: stops per car, all stops, over the races the model is fitted on.
  • An unseen weather or start-tyre level uses its nearest seen level (wet → mixed → dry; WET → INTERMEDIATE).
  • The model counts the stops of cars that keep running; a car out before its first stop is drawn separately, at the share of such cars in the season’s earlier races.
  • Race weather comes from Open-Meteo’s Historical Forecast API, because its Previous Runs API has rain probability for only two of 2026’s first 17 rounds (Miami and Canada): the rain chance p is the higher precipitation probability of the race-start hour and the next, and the weights are dry 1 - p, mixed p/2 and wet p/2.
  • That API keeps the latest forecast for each hour, fetched after the races, not the forecast made the day before, so the backtest’s weather is better than a real day-before forecast and its scores are optimistic.
  • Tyre wear is not a model input: the race wear slope is often negative, because the car getting lighter as fuel burns outweighs the wear.
  • The incident chance rests on few races: it is the share of earlier races at the circuit and earlier races of the season with a safety car or red flag (a VSC does not count), and the backtest tests it only through its season half, because no backtest round is at a circuit with earlier races in the data.
  • A running car makes at least the race minimum of stops: 1 in a dry race, more where a circuit’s rules require it, and 1 in a mixed or wet race; the model counts the stops above it.
  • The team’s two cars usually make the same number of stops: in each draw they share one random draw for their extra stops with a chance set from the season’s earlier races where both finished, and each car keeps its own spread.

Data and method

Every number on this page comes from f1_analytics.analysis.pit_stops, run on the local DuckDB file built from the committed raw data, with the scenarios read from data/processed/scenarios/ and checked against the database when the page is rendered. The model is a Poisson count model of each running car’s stops above the race minimum, fitted on races before this one, with 10,000 seeded draws.
