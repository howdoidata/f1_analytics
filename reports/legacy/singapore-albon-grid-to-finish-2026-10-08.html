1 · Type of analysis and why

Kaggle CSVs ──┐ ├──→ DuckDB ──→ combined_results ──→ Monte Carlo ──→ estimate Jolpica API ──┘ (season 2026) (25 × 20,000 races)

Method: Monte Carlo simulation. The race is simulated 500,000 times. Each simulated race draws every uncertain ingredient at random from real 2026 results, and the answer is read from where the outcomes pile up.

Why a simulation, not a single formulaWhat it means here
The outcome is two different races in oneWhen Albon finishes he averages +2.3 places; when he retires, −2.7. An average of the two describes a race that rarely happens
His grid slot is unknownQualifying has not run and the starting slot changes the answer, so each simulated race draws a grid slot too
Only 16 races of evidenceThe simulation carries that uncertainty through to the result instead of hiding it in one number

Three draws per simulated race

DrawSource
1 · Grid slotHis 16 starting slots this season, recent races weighted more (weight halves every 6 races)
2 · Retires or finishesHis record of 6 non-finishes in 16 blended with the field rate of 21%, giving a 32% retirement chance that itself varies from race to race
3 · Places gained or lostIf he finishes: half the draws from his own 10 finishes, half from other 2026 finishers who started within two slots of him. If he retires: from 2026 retirements from a similar slot

6 · Caveats

  • Qualifying has not run. Grid slot is the largest single input and is simulated, not known.
  • Round 16 is unresolved. Jolpica returned a full round 16 result labelled "Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia", while the calendar table lists round 16 as Singapore on 2026-10-11. The base case includes it; excluding it keeps the median at +1.
  • No Singapore-specific or weather adjustment. Safety cars, rain and penalties are represented only to the extent they shaped 2026 results so far.
  • This is a pre-race estimate, not a computed answer. It should be reconciled against the classified result once Singapore data lands.
